# QUI — Landing Page Specification

**Status:** CANONICAL product/marketing specification  
**Date:** 2026-08-19  
**Scope:** public landing page narrative, sections, messages, CTA, visual assets  
**Target:** `qui.social`

## 0. Design intent

The retained dark mockup defines the **emotional and narrative direction** of the landing page.

It does **not** define the default application UI.

The landing page should feel:

- cinematic and human;
- high-contrast and memorable;
- centered on real faces and real activities;
- energetic without looking crypto/cyberpunk;
- trustworthy without looking institutional;
- ambitious enough to feel like a new social network, not a local utility.

Use the canonical QUI brand palette. Dark marketing backgrounds should be derived from Ink, with Coral as the primary light/energy accent.

---

# 1. Landing objective

A first-time visitor should understand within ~10 seconds:

1. QUI is a social network;
2. it is for real people;
3. accounts have equal structural standing;
4. it is about what people do and can do together;
5. it starts locally/around the user;
6. verification is lightweight and can use an approved ID/trusted-invitation path;
7. the product is not another bot/influencer/pay-for-reach network.

The page should then prove the concept with product UI, not rely only on manifesto copy.

---

# 2. Global page structure

### Recommended order — BASELINE

1. Header
2. Hero: social network for real/equal/active humans
3. Proof strip: Real / Equal / Active / Local
4. Product loop: Discover → Signal → Do something
5. Discover section
6. Profiles/practices section
7. Signals/projects/activities section
8. Equal-standing explanation
9. Trust/verification explanation
10. Cities/local-first explanation
11. Open-source/build-in-public trust section
12. Final CTA
13. Footer

On mobile, preserve the same narrative but reduce copy and avoid overlong cinematic sections.

---

# 3. Header

### Required

Left:

- QUI wordmark;
- optional subtle `qui.social` text on wide desktop only.

Right:

- `How it works`
- `Principles` or `Why QUI`
- `Open source`
- primary CTA: `Get early access`

### Mobile

- logo left;
- CTA right;
- compact menu for secondary links.

Avoid a large navigation taxonomy before the product is live.

---

# 4. Hero

## 4.1 Preferred headline — BASELINE

> **Social network for everyone.**

Visually emphasize `everyone` using Coral, as in the retained concept.

This line communicates broad access while the following copy explains that QUI is not open to fake/bot identities.

## 4.2 Preferred subhead — CANONICAL BASELINE

> **Verified real people. Equal standing. Share what you do, your skills and your projects. Find people around you and build things together — online and offline.**

Copy may be shortened responsively but must preserve the four ideas: real, equal, active, local.

## 4.3 Trust line

Preferred safe marketing formulation:

> **Built for people, not bots, fake accounts or troll farms.**

Do not make technically absolute claims such as “zero trolls forever” unless the product can substantiate them.

## 4.4 CTA

Primary:

> **Get early access**

Secondary:

> **See how it works**

Secondary CTA scrolls to the Discover → Signal → real-world loop rather than opening a generic feature grid.

## 4.5 Hero visual

The retained dark composition is the conceptual reference:

- one central human face;
- several diverse surrounding people;
- visible relationship/network lines;
- small concept labels/icons around the network;
- geographic/local texture or city lights;
- a subtle visual bridge between digital network and physical world.

### Required message labels

Use a restrained subset, not all simultaneously on small screens:

- `Verified human`
- `Trusted invitation`
- `Equal standing`
- `Share skills`
- `Build together`
- `Real connections`
- `Local-first`

Do not make the network diagram look like a surveillance map or a blockchain graph.

---

# 5. Four-pillar proof strip

Immediately after the hero, present four concise pillars.

## REAL

**Headline:** `Real humans`  
**Copy:** `Identity or trusted verification paths designed to keep fake accounts out while storing as little sensitive data as possible.`

## EQUAL

**Headline:** `Equal standing`  
**Copy:** `No KOL boost. No purchased organic reach. No inherited privilege from follower counts elsewhere.`

## ACTIVE

**Headline:** `Built for doing`  
**Copy:** `Share what you practice, teach, build or want to join — not only what you consume.`

## LOCAL

**Headline:** `People around you`  
**Copy:** `Discover people, projects, skills and activities through real geographic context, without exposing live people coordinates.`

---

# 6. Product loop section

### Headline

> **From discovery to doing.**

### Narrative

Use real UI mockups/components to show:

```text
Discover someone / something interesting
        ↓
See what they do, know or are building
        ↓
Send or answer a Signal
        ↓
Join, learn, teach, build, help or meet
```

### Supporting line

> **QUI is designed so closing the app can be a successful outcome.**

This should be a distinctive product statement, not hidden in the footer.

---

# 7. Discover section

### Headline

> **Discover people worth knowing because of what they do.**

### Show

- local visual posts/Reels;
- people cards;
- projects/practices;
- city context;
- trending/breakout labels where appropriate;
- a direct route from content to profile/practice/Signal.

### Explain

The feed can be entertaining, but the user should be able to understand:

- who made this;
- where it belongs;
- what this person does;
- what action is possible next.

Avoid heroing follower counts/likes as the primary social proof.

---

# 8. Profiles section

### Headline

> **A profile about what you do — not your follower résumé.**

Show fields such as:

- practices;
- skills;
- can teach;
- can help with;
- wants to learn;
- current project/activity;
- selected social identities;
- city/local attachment.

Use factual contribution/activity indicators rather than universal reputation scores.

---

# 9. Signals section

### Headline

> **Give people a reason to connect.**

Show typed Signals such as:

- Ask;
- Offer;
- Learn;
- Teach;
- Join;
- Event;
- Project.

Example copy:

> `Looking for two people to help restore a small sailing boat this Saturday.`

> `I can teach beginner street photography — one-hour walk this week.`

> `Building a local music session. Looking for a guitarist and percussionist.`

The examples should feel real and active, not like a classifieds board.

---

# 10. Equal standing section

### Headline

> **Reach is earned. It isn't bought or inherited.**

### Core explanation

> Every ordinary account starts with the same structural standing. Being famous elsewhere, buying a subscription or being labeled an influencer does not grant an organic-distribution advantage. Great content can still earn broader reach through relevance and genuine response.

### Visual

Show a simple distribution progression:

```text
Local → City → Region → Country
```

paired with:

> `Same starting line. Wider reach comes from what resonates.`

Do not use imagery of balances/scales so heavily that QUI looks like a political equality campaign.

---

# 11. Trust / verification section

### Headline

> **Trust without turning your identity into the product.**

### Explain

- lightweight third-party ID verification can be one trust path;
- trusted invitation/cooptation can be another approved path once the trust model is finalized;
- QUI should store minimal attestations rather than unnecessary ID document material;
- profile/public APIs do not expose KYC documents;
- local presence is a separate proof from identity.

### User-facing wording

Prefer:

- `Verified with ID`
- `Verified through a trusted invitation`

Avoid:

- `KYC tier 1/2/3` in consumer-facing UI;
- banking/compliance language;
- claims of “zero-knowledge KYC” unless technically true.

---

# 12. Cities / local-first section

### Headline

> **Start with the people you could actually do something with.**

### Explain

- QUI can exist in any city;
- users can explore cities globally;
- local publishing/distribution may require legitimate local attachment;
- QUI does not need or expose persistent people-location trails;
- content can break out beyond its starting city when it genuinely resonates.

### Visual

Use a city switcher and real application cards rather than a map of live people.

---

# 13. Open source / build in public section

### Headline

> **Open source. Built in public.**

### Copy

> QUI asks users to trust important rules about identity, reach, privacy and local discovery. The product is therefore being built openly, with its code and non-sensitive product decisions inspectable on GitHub.

Secondary line:

> Security secrets, private user data and active vulnerability details stay private by design.

### CTA

- `View on GitHub`
- optional `Follow the build`

Do not make open source the primary consumer pitch; it is a trust multiplier.

---

# 14. Final CTA

### Preferred

> **Find out who's around — and what you could do together.**

Primary:

> **Get early access**

Secondary:

> **Follow the build**

Optional small copy:

> `Free for ordinary users.`

---

# 15. Landing visual asset plan

Agents should build the page with a mix of:

1. canonical SVG logo assets;
2. high-quality portraits/activity imagery;
3. actual QUI UI screenshots/components;
4. small line-icon illustrations;
5. subtle network/geographic graphic motifs;
6. one strong dark cinematic hero composition;
7. light product UI sections below the hero to prove the product.

### Avoid

- a page that is only one giant generated illustration;
- fake testimonials;
- fake user counts;
- fake city activity metrics;
- fake press logos;
- invented “10,000 users nearby” numbers;
- overuse of neon/orange glow;
- generic SaaS feature-card walls.

---

# 16. Responsive behavior

### Mobile

- hero copy above visual;
- one primary CTA visible without scrolling excessively;
- surrounding-person network visual simplified to 3–4 faces;
- proof pillars become horizontally scrollable cards or a 2×2 grid;
- real UI mockups take priority over decorative diagrams below hero;
- no tiny network-label text embedded into raster imagery.

### Desktop

- two-column or asymmetric hero allowed;
- central-person network visual can occupy 50–65% of viewport width;
- preserve strong whitespace in light product sections.

---

# 17. Landing acceptance criteria

The landing page is ready for owner review only if a visitor can answer:

- [ ] What is QUI?
- [ ] Why are the accounts more trustworthy?
- [ ] What does “equal” actually mean?
- [ ] Why would I post here rather than only watch content?
- [ ] How is it local without being a people-tracking map?
- [ ] How do Discover and Signals connect?
- [ ] Is the core consumer product free?
- [ ] Is the project open source/build in public?

And visually:

- [ ] Hero follows the dark cinematic direction without turning the entire product dark.
- [ ] Product UI shown below uses the light canonical design system.
- [ ] Coral/Ink canonical brand tokens are used.
- [ ] Humans and activities dominate over metrics and technology graphics.
- [ ] No fake social proof is present.
